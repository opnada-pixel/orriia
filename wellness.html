The world recordProposed

Measured, not asserted.

ORIA proposes to open as the world's largest thermal bathing facility — a Guinness World Records™ title attempt, defined with the record body before design freeze.

Why it belongs to the concept

ORIA builds the thermal world first and attaches everything else to it. The record would measure exactly that — so the headline and the product are the same thing.

The concept

What is actually being proposed.

01What is proposed

A Guinness World Records™ title for the thermal facility at ORIA — applied for, adjudicated and verified in the normal way, before the resort opens.

02What is measured

One figure: the built area given over to thermal water, heat and recovery. At design target, 30,000 m² of pools, thermal rooms, saunas, hydro and recovery spaces.

03Why a new category

The existing record measures a whole spa resort's site. ORIA would measure only the thermal world inside it — a different thing, so it needs its own definition.

The two figures

Why the smaller number is the point.

Drawn to true relative area, one sits inside the other. 176,284 m² is a resort; 30,000 m² is the bathing facility inside one. Set side by side without that distinction, it would be comparing a city to a building in it.

The figures are a comparison, not a claim.

176,284 m²Current title — a whole resort, measured as a site
30,000 m²ORIA — the thermal facility only
The route

A record is won in the specification, not in the announcement.

Five steps, in order. None has been completed yet.

04 Build and evidence
Survey, drawings and records assembled as the project is constructed.
05 Independent adjudication
An official adjudicator measures and verifies on site.
